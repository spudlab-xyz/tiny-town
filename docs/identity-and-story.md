# Tiny Town identity and story

Status: production copy and visual direction for implementation review
Policy reviewed: `spudlab-xyz/operations@5e39fd0c4f145a2fe993e1a314a680c3d3bc2e7c`

Tiny Town is a playful open-source place shaped through proposals. It should feel like a lived-in miniature settlement, not a generic project dashboard: warm, legible, gently odd, and clear about what is story versus real project activity.

## Positioning

**Short description**
A tiny shared town where open-source ideas become places, people, and local lore.

**One-line invitation**
Come for a wander. Propose what the town becomes next.

**Story promise**
Every part of Tiny Town should reward curiosity. A label helps you find your way; a resident gives the place a point of view; a notice suggests what might happen next. The fiction creates atmosphere, while the interface stays honest about the real state of the project.

Do not describe unmerged proposals as built, seeded examples as community submissions, or fictional notices as real project commitments.

## Founding story

Use this as the canonical long version:

> Tiny Town began with a signpost and an empty patch of ground. Nobody agreed which way the first arrow should point, so they planted it upright and called the spot Town Square. A workshop appeared beside it, then a garden, an archive, and a lookout on the hill. Each new corner started the same way: someone noticed what was missing and made a proposal.
>
> The town is still small enough to cross on foot and unfinished enough to change your mind about. Its streets hold experiments, useful oddities, and room for the next good idea. Visitors are welcome to explore what is here, read the local notices, and suggest what Tiny Town should become next.

Compact version:

> Tiny Town began with one undecided signpost. It grows whenever someone notices what is missing and proposes a new piece of the map.

## Visual direction

### Creative idea: handmade civic miniature

Combine the clarity of public wayfinding with the charm of a model village. Geometry is simple and slightly irregular: paper-cut rooflines, hand-set signs, garden plots, footpaths, and small practical details. The character comes from composition and copy, not visual clutter.

### Palette

| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---:|---|
| Ink | `#243229` | Primary text, outlines |
| Paper | `#FFF8E7` | Main background |
| Moss | `#47704E` | Primary actions, landscape |
| Brick | `#B9573F` | Roofs, active accents |
| Marigold | `#E9A23B` | Highlights, notice pins |
| Pond | `#4E8291` | Water, secondary accents |
| Plum | `#72566F` | Archive and evening accents |

Ink on Paper and Paper on Moss are the default high-contrast pairs. Do not use hue alone to communicate state; pair color with text, shape, or an icon. Keep body copy on quiet, solid surfaces rather than textured scenery.

### Type and shape

- Headings: a friendly slab serif or sturdy rounded serif, used sparingly.
- Interface and body: a highly legible humanist sans serif.
- Labels: sentence case. Avoid all-caps except for very short map keys.
- Corners: modestly rounded, as if cut from card—not pill-shaped everywhere.
- Illustration: flat color, dark economical outlines, one texture layer at most. Favor an oblique map view over faux-realistic 3D.
- Motion: small environmental loops only when they clarify life or state. Respect reduced-motion preferences.

### Motif and mark

The recurring motif is the **three-way signpost**: one arrow for what exists, one for what is proposed, and one left blank for what comes next. It can anchor the wordmark, favicon exploration, loading state, or empty-state art. Do not finalize a logo from this direction without a separate design review.

## Voice

Tiny Town sounds like a capable neighbor with a dry smile.

- **Warm, not sugary:** “Take a look around” rather than “Welcome to our magical little world!”
- **Specific, not grand:** name a place, object, or next action.
- **Inviting, not presumptive:** “Propose a place” rather than “Join our thriving community.”
- **Playful, not cryptic:** atmosphere must never obscure navigation or project state.
- **Honest, not performative:** distinguish seeded examples, open proposals, accepted work, and released work.

Preferred interface verbs: **explore, visit, read, propose, build, revise, release**.
Avoid: **revolutionize, bustling community, beloved, official roadmap, guaranteed, coming soon** unless independently supported.

## The five places

### 1. The Signpost — Town Square

**Map label:** The Signpost
**Short description:** The town’s starting point, where paths and proposals meet.
**Detail copy:** The first landmark in Tiny Town has never quite settled which way is forward. Use the Signpost to orient yourself, see what is established, and find the route for proposing something new.
**Visual cue:** A three-arrow post in a pale stone circle, surrounded by an uneven ring of stools.

### 2. Patchwork Works — Workshop

**Map label:** Patchwork Works
**Short description:** A practical workshop for ideas that need testing.
**Detail copy:** Half workbench, half cupboard of useful leftovers, Patchwork Works is where a rough notion earns its hinges. Plans are pinned up, prototypes are tried, and nothing is called finished before it works.
**Visual cue:** Brick roof, broad doors, timber scraps, and one small test contraption outside.

### 3. The Long Table — Garden

**Map label:** The Long Table
**Short description:** A garden and shared table for slower conversations.
**Detail copy:** Beans climb the trellis beside a table built from five different kinds of wood. Residents bring questions here when they need more daylight, more viewpoints, or simply somewhere to set down a mug.
**Visual cue:** Mossy beds, marigold dots, mismatched chairs, and a long central table.

### 4. Small Hours Archive — Library

**Map label:** Small Hours Archive
**Short description:** The record of what changed, and why.
**Detail copy:** The Archive keeps maps, field notes, and careful accounts of the town’s revisions. Its drawers make room for abandoned routes as well as working ones, because a useful history includes the ideas that changed.
**Visual cue:** Plum door, many narrow windows, map drawers, and a clock without a bell.

### 5. Weatherstep — Lookout

**Map label:** Weatherstep
**Short description:** A hilltop lookout for seeing the whole town at once.
**Detail copy:** From Weatherstep, footpaths become patterns and empty plots become possibilities. It is a place to take stock—not a promise that everything on the horizon will be built.
**Visual cue:** Pond-blue telescope, windsock, low stone steps, and generous open sky.

## Residents

Residents are fictional guides, not representations of maintainers, contributors, users, or automated agents.

### Mara Venn — Sign painter

Mara painted the first arrow at the Signpost and has been revising it ever since. She believes a useful sign tells you where you are before it tells you where to go. Find her in Town Square with a ruler, a paint-stained sleeve, and a tactful opinion about labels.

### Kit Bramble — Workshop keeper

Kit runs Patchwork Works and keeps every screw in the wrong tin for surprisingly good reasons. They ask three questions of a new idea: What is it for? Who can use it? What happens when it breaks?

### Iona Peat — Gardener and host

Iona tends the Long Table’s garden, where no two rows are quite parallel. She is patient with seedlings and impatient with vague invitations. If a conversation needs another chair, Iona finds one.

### Oren Vale — Archivist

Oren looks after the Small Hours Archive and writes dates on everything, including lunch. He records decisions without sanding off the uncertainty, then leaves a clean margin for whoever learns something next.

### Tavi Rook — Lookout

Tavi walks up to Weatherstep before the kettle boils. She can spot a new roof from across town, but she will not call it complete until someone has checked the door. Her forecasts are concise: “Clear enough to test.”

### Pip — Parcel crow

Pip carries notes between the five places and occasionally delivers them to the correct one. The blue thread around one ankle is not a badge of office; it was simply too interesting to leave behind.

## Noticeboard copy

These are fictional, seeded notices for the initial experience. Display the visible prefix **Town seed** on each card and expose the full disclosure in an accessible label or help text.

**Seed disclosure:** “This is a fictional example included with Tiny Town. It is not a real community post, proposal, event, or commitment.”

1. **Town seed · Stool audit**
   The Square has seven stools and eight opinions about where they belong. Mara requests one volunteer with a measuring tape and no strong feelings about circles.

2. **Town seed · Hinges wanted**
   Patchwork Works needs two quiet hinges for the map cupboard. Squeaky hinges will be considered if they have a compelling explanation.

3. **Town seed · Table gap**
   One plank at the Long Table is exactly wide enough to swallow a teaspoon. Iona proposes a repair before the next pot of tea.

4. **Town seed · Archive labeling hour**
   Oren is sorting the drawer marked “temporary.” It contains records from three different eras and one biscuit recipe.

5. **Town seed · Visibility report**
   Weatherstep can currently see four roofs, two kites, and weather. Tavi will update the count after the mist lifts.

6. **Town seed · Found note**
   Pip found a folded sketch near the Signpost. It may show a bridge, a bookshelf, or an ambitious sandwich.

7. **Town seed · Open plot**
   The small plot east of the garden has no assigned use. Explore the town first, then propose what would make the map more useful or more delightful.

8. **Town seed · Path test**
   A new stepping-stone route is ready to try. Please report the wobbly stone, not merely that stones can wobble.

## Seeded-demo labels and states

Use these exact labels consistently in filters, cards, map legends, and detail views:

| Label | Meaning | Suggested UI copy |
|---|---|---|
| `Town seed` | Fictional example shipped with the demo | Included example—not community activity |
| `Proposed` | A real suggestion not yet accepted | Proposed; not part of the town yet |
| `Accepted` | Approved for implementation | Accepted; work may still be in progress |
| `Built` | Present in the current product | Built and available to explore |
| `Archived` | Retained for history, not active | Archived; kept as part of the record |

Never use `Resident` for a real account role unless the product deliberately defines and implements that role. Never generate proposal counts, contributor counts, dates, usernames, votes, or status changes as decorative content.

## Release-history language

The release history is a factual product record with a light town metaphor, not fictional lore. Use **release** for a verifiable published release and **update** for other shipped changes.

**Empty state**

> No releases are recorded yet. When a version is published, its date, source link, and verified changes will appear here.

**Section introduction**

> A record of what changed in Tiny Town. Each entry links to its source and separates new places, improvements, and repairs.

**Release entry template**

> **[Version or update name]** · [YYYY-MM-DD]
> [One factual sentence describing the user-visible change.]
> **New places:** [Verified additions, or “None.”]
> **Improvements:** [Verified improvements, or “None.”]
> **Repairs:** [Verified fixes, or “None.”]
> [View source / View release]

Do not use “latest,” “today,” or relative dates without rendering an exact date nearby. Do not imply that a proposal shipped merely because it was discussed or accepted. Do not fabricate version numbers or backfill a founding release.

## Implementation notes

- Keep story content separate from real activity data. A useful minimum record is `id`, `kind`, `title`, `summary`, `body`, `status`, `isSeeded`, and `sourceUrl` where a real source exists.
- Treat the five places and six residents as versioned editorial content; stable IDs should not be derived from display names.
- Seeded notices should default to `isSeeded: true` and must not have invented authors, timestamps, votes, or source URLs.
- The map legend must explain `Built`, `Proposed`, and `Town seed` without relying on color alone.
- Provide alt text for meaningful illustrations. Decorative scenery should use empty alt text.
- Validate final type choices and palette combinations against applicable contrast and responsive-layout checks during implementation.
